What belongs under the balance

What's there now is a 16-point sample of end-of-day balances with no axis, no labels and no hover. For most people that's a flat line with a step at payday — the widest element on the screen carrying the least information.

The test I'd apply: a balance number already answers "how much do I have." Anything under it has to answer a different question — otherwise it's decoration. The two that actually matter here are "am I above my floor?" and "how long does this last?" A bare trend line answers neither.

01

Just the number

no chart · baseline for comparison

Hero figure, delta, and two range chips. Nothing to misread, nothing to maintain, and the number gets the full width. Worth seeing first because everything below has to beat it.

△ Honest and clean, but throws away the one thing a chart is good at: showing you didn't notice a slide.

02

Balance against your floor

line vs baseline · Δ to target

Same line, but now it means something: your buffer (stop_at + bills still due) is drawn as a floor, the band below it is shaded, and the gap between the line and that floor is your real spendable room. The endpoint carries a label so you can read a value — which the current sparkline can't do.

✓ My recommendation. Keeps the visual you already have and gives it a job. The line dipping toward the floor is a genuine signal.

03

Daily flow

diverging bars from zero

One thin bar per day of spending for 30 days, with payday marked as a flag. You read rhythm instead of a cumulative curve — the weekend clusters, the quiet stretches, the one big week. A running balance hides all of that inside its slope. (First pass drew income as a bar too; a $2,710 payday against $20–80 days flattened every expense bar to a hairline, so income became an annotation instead — one scale, one meaning.)

✓ Strong second. Genuinely more informative than the line, and the shape is unmistakable at a glance.

04

Runway

meter · one ratio against a limit

Drops the chart entirely and answers the question people actually open the app for: at your current burn rate, how long does this last? One meter, one sentence, no axis to read.

✓ The most useful single thing you could put here — and the cheapest to build.

05

Pay cycle

progress + per-day allowance

Where you are between paychecks, and what the remaining balance works out to per day until the next one. Turns the balance into a pace rather than a total.

△ Good, but overlaps your Weekly Planner. I'd only use it if you want the dashboard to replace that page's headline.

06

The current line, done properly

labels + hover readout

If you like the line as-is, this is the minimum to make it legible: min/max/end labelled, a real 30-day span, and a crosshair readout on touch. The reference is explicit that an SVG chart ships a hover layer by default — right now there's nothing to interrogate.

△ Fixes the legibility, still answers "how much do I have" twice.

If it were my call

  1. 04 Runway as the default — it answers the question the number can't, in one line of text and a meter.
  2. 02 Balance vs floor if you want to keep a chart there. It's the current line plus a baseline, so it's a small change with a real payoff.
  3. 03 Daily flow if you'd rather see behaviour than totals — best "aha" of the six.
  4. Don't stack them. One answer under the hero; the rest belong on Insights.

Whichever you pick, the floor value comes from data you already have — weekly_plan.stop_at plus bills still due — so none of these need new inputs from you.
